# Specializations

Specializations represent a character's focused expertise under a specific set of circumstances. They modify how Trait Dice function when applicable.

---

## Gaining Specializations
- **Level 1:** Gain 1 Combat Specialization and 1 Role-Play Specialization.
- **Non-magical characters:** Gain 4 additional specializations at character creation.
- **Class modifier:** "Additional Specialization" may be selected during class definition.
- **Every level divisible by 4:** Gain +1 Specialization.

---

## Defining a Specialization
When a specialization is gained, the player describes a specific circumstance. This must be:
- **Approved by the GM**
- **Broad enough** to come up regularly
- **Narrow enough** not to apply constantly
- **Non-overlapping** — no two specializations may be defined with the intent to cover the same roll. For example, a specialization that applies "when wielding sharp weapons" and another that applies "when using a specific sword" cannot coexist — the second is a subset of the first.

### Examples of Acceptable Scope
- "When fighting at night."
- "When defending allies."
- "When negotiating for money."
- "When attacking goblins."

---

## Mechanical Effect
When a specialization applies to a roll:
- Roll the relevant **Trait Die twice** and add both results.
- The d20 is rolled once normally.
- The Trait Value is added once normally.
- **Only the Trait Die is rolled twice.**

---

## Stacking
If multiple specializations apply to the same roll, each one adds an additional Trait Die roll.

**Example:** 3 specializations apply → roll the Trait Die 4 times and add all results.

There is no cap on stacking unless the GM rules otherwise.

---

## Limitations
Specializations:
- Do not grant automatic success.
- Do not bypass core action limits.
- Do not override action economy unless another rule explicitly allows it.
- Must still fit within what can reasonably occur in one turn.

---

## Interaction with Repeated Attempt Penalty
The repeated attempt penalty still applies even when a specialization is active. Specializations do not negate the −5 stacking penalty for retrying the same approach on the same problem.
