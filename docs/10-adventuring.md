# Adventuring

Non-combat resolution governs:
- Social interaction
- Stealth
- Investigation
- Chases
- Extended tasks
- Environmental challenges
- Narrative obstacles
- Downtime

---

## Opposed Rolls
Used when another character is actively resisting the player's action.

**Examples:** Arm wrestling, sneaking past an alert sentry, persuading an unwilling target, competitive actions between characters.

All involved parties roll using appropriate traits for their method of "winning." The character with the highest result determines the outcome — and may choose to lose if narratively appropriate or desirable.

---

## Standard Difficulty Checks
Used when a target is present but not actively resisting.

**Examples:**
- Sneaking past a creature not on active alert
- Moving an unconscious creature
- Environmental tasks (picking a lock, pushing a boulder)

The GM assigns a difficulty based on established benchmarks.

---

## Specialized Tools

### Basic Equipment
Some tasks require basic equipment merely to attempt at all. The equipment doesn't make the task easier — it makes attempting it possible.

> *Example: A key doesn't make unlocking a door easier; it makes it possible.*

### Advantage-Granting Equipment
Other equipment grants a mechanical bonus. When a character is trained to use such equipment and applies it in the appropriate circumstance, they gain **advantage** on the roll.

The circumstances that grant advantage must be defined when the equipment is acquired.

**Examples:**
- Dedicated thieves' tools for lockpicking
- A hooked climbing rope for climbing
- A compass used for navigation

---

## Social Conflict
Social conflict follows normal resolution rules. The GM determines whether rolls require opposition or standard difficulties. Any trait may be used if deemed appropriate by the GM. Most common social traits: Charisma, Wisdom, Knowledge.

---

## Extended Tasks
For complex or long-term objectives, the GM determines:
- Number of rolls required
- Time required per roll
- Stakes for failure

Success may require:
- Multiple cumulative successes
- Sequential successes
- Success within a time constraint

Failure may:
- Escalate consequences
- Increase difficulty
- Introduce new complications

---

## Downtime
Downtime is primarily narrative, handled collaboratively by players and the GM. The GM may call for rolls if players are working toward a specific production or achievement during downtime. The GM determines the required number and difficulty of those rolls.

---

## Rest and Recovery

An adventuring day ends when the party sleeps. Only one Rest may be taken between each Sleep.

### Rest *(once per adventuring day)*
- Recover **half** of missing Resolve.
- Recover **all** Resonance.

### Sleep *(end of adventuring day)*
- Recover **all** missing Resolve.
- Recover **all** Resonance.
- Remove **one ongoing condition** (unless the condition states otherwise).

---

## GM Authority
The GM determines:
- When a roll is required
- The relevant trait
- The Difficulty
- Whether the action is possible
- Whether a single roll or multiple rolls are required

All non-combat resolution flows through GM adjudication guided by established difficulty benchmarks.
