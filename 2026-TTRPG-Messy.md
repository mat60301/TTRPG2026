NEW TTRPG – COMPLETE RULEBOOK (TEXT EDITION)

DESIGN PRINCIPLES
1) Players have more fun when they succeed.
2) Players have more fun when they actively participate.
3) Players enjoy rolling dice, especially multiple dice.
4) The risk of failure must be real in order to appreciate success.
5) Complexity is welcome; excessive amounts of rules are overbearing.

CORE RESOLUTION MECHANIC
A player declares what they intend to do.
The GM determines:
Whether a roll is required.
The difficulty of successfully completing the task.
Whether the action requires a single roll or multiple rolls.
How long it may take the player to complete the task.


CORE ROLL MECHANIC
Unless otherwise noted, when a roll is required the player rolls:
1d20 + Associated Trait Die + Associated Trait Value + External/Additional Modifiers

The roll is considered a success if the result meets or exceeds the assigned difficulty of the task.

If the calculated value of the roll is less than the assigned difficulty of the task then the roll is considered a failure.

*Note*
Failure should always produce a narrative result. The action may not achieve the player’s intent, but something should still happen. As an example, consider that, if a player throws a fireball at a creature and misses, then the creature may be spared the brunt of the attack… but there’s still a fireball hurtling through the room…

Difficulty Benchmarks
These values are tools for the GM and do not scale with level. They have been calculated based on the mathematical odds built into the system.
Very Easy: 7
Easy: 11
Average: 16
Hard: 20
Very Hard: 24
Impossible: 28


Tasks do not become harder as players level up. Players simply encounter more difficult tasks.

Repeated Attempt Penalty
If a character attempts to solve a problem and fails:
Second attempt: −5 penalty
Third attempt: −10 penalty
Penalty continues stacking (−15, −20, etc.)


This penalty applies when:
The same trait is used repeatedly…
On the same problem…
Without a meaningful change in approach.


Advantage / Disadvantage
Advantage allows the player to Roll the d20 twice and take the higher result.
Disadvantage forces the player to Roll the d20 twice and take the lower result.

Players receive 20 trait points.
No more than two traits may begin at 4.
No trait may begin above 4.
Traits may begin at 0.

TRAIT DICE
All trait dice begin at d4.
On even levels, a player may upgrade one trait die:
d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12
Trait dice may never exceed d12.


USING TRAITS

Player intent
GM adjudication

Physical Attacks

DEFENSE CALCULATIONS
In order to land a hit, the value of an attacker’s roll must meet or exceed the relevant defense of the attacker’s target.
Physical Defense = Endurance + Balance + Toughness + Level
Mental Defense = Willpower + Awareness + Empathy + Level

DAMAGE MITIGATION
Mitigation reduces the damage suffered by a hit target. Mitigation applies to the appropriate incoming damage type (predominantly physical or mental), and does NOT apply to ongoing damage unless otherwise stated.
Physical Mitigation = Balance + Toughness
Mental Mitigation = Awareness + Empathy


TRAITS & MOVEMENT
Combat Speed is determined by Speed:
Base Speed: 20 feet
Every 2 points in Speed (rounded down) adds 5 feet


Example:
Speed 0–1 → 20 ft
Speed 2–3 → 25 ft
Speed 4–5 → 30 ft
Speed 6–7 → 35 ft


Out-of-combat speed = double combat speed

TRAITS & RESOURCES
Resolve = (Endurance × Willpower) + Level
Resonance = Empathy + Balance + Level

CHARACTER CREATION
Character creation consists of:
Selecting Species
Defining Class
Choosing Magic (or not)
Assigning Traits
Selecting Specializations

1. SPECIES
A player may choose any species.
Species do not grant mechanical benefits at character creation.
A player may choose a species that would normally provide advantages (e.g., wings, supernatural features), but those benefits are not active at creation.
Any exceptional species traits must be explained narratively and may not provide mechanical benefits at level 1.


2. CLASS
Players define their class narratively.
Classes are not pre-written archetypes.
Each character then selects 3 Class Mechanic Modifiers from the list below:
Increase to movement speed
+1 to physical damage mitigation
+1 to mental damage mitigation
+1 to physical damage
+1 to magical damage
+1 to physical or magical attacks
+2 to Resolve
+1 to Resonance
Select an additional specialization
Weapon Training
Armor Training
Charm Training


Weapon Training
Each instance:
Applies to one weapon category (Simple, Light, Standard, Heavy, Brutal)
Grants +1 accuracy
Grants +1 damage
Only applies when using that category


Armor Training
Each instance:
Applies to one armor category
Grants +1 to that armor’s defense modifier

Charm Training
Each instance:
Applies to one charm category
Grants +1 to that charm’s defense modifier

4. MAGIC (Optional)
At character creation, a player chooses whether they use magic.

If Magical
The player must:
Choose a Magical Domain that loosely defines the nature of the magic they can attempt.
Define 2 Foundational Effects
Must fall within the chosen domain.
Must be relatively minor.
Must be approved by the GM.
Typically one combat effect and one role-play effect (not required).


Foundational effects:
Do not cost Resonance to use at baseline.
Can be expanded using Resonance.
Must be narrow enough to avoid universal application, but broad enough to have multiple uses.


If Non-Magical
If a character chooses not to use magic:
They do not select a magical domain.
They do not define foundational effects.
Instead, they gain 2 additional specializations.


5. SPECIALIZATIONS
At Level 1:
Gain 1 Combat Specialization
Gain 1 Role-Play Specialization


Specializations:
Define a circumstance approved by the GM.
Must be broad enough to be used often.
Must not be so broad that they apply constantly.


When applicable:
Roll the relevant trait die twice and add both results.


Specializations stack if:
Multiple apply to the same roll.

LEVEL ADVANCEMENT
There is no level cap.
Trait dice may never exceed d12.
Level advancement provides different benefits depending on whether the new level is odd, even, or divisible by specific values.

ODD LEVELS
(Level 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, etc.)
Gain +1 Trait Point.
This point may be assigned to any of the 12 traits.
Trait values may exceed 4 after level 1.
There is no upper limit to trait value through advancement.


EVEN LEVELS
(Level 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, etc.)
Upgrade one Trait Die.
Trait die progression:
d4 → d6 → d8 → d10 → d12
Trait dice cannot exceed d12.


LEVELS DIVISIBLE BY 3
(Level 3, 6, 9, 12, etc.)
Gain +1 Foundational Magical Effect, or…
Gain an additional class modifier


LEVELS DIVISIBLE BY 4
(Level 4, 8, 12, 16, etc.)
Gain +1 Specialization.



SPECIALIZATIONS
Specializations represent a character’s focused expertise in a specific circumstance.
They modify how Trait Dice function when applicable.

DEFINING A SPECIALIZATION
When gained, the player must:
Describe a specific circumstance.
The circumstance must be approved by the GM.


Examples of acceptable scope:
“When fighting at night.”
“When defending allies.”
“When negotiating for money.”
“When attacking goblins.”


MECHANICAL EFFECT
When a specialization applies:
Roll the relevant Trait Die twice and add both results.
This replaces rolling the Trait Die once.
The d20 is rolled normally.
The Trait Value is added normally.
Only the Trait Die is rolled twice.

STACKING RULES
If multiple specializations apply to the same roll:
All applicable specializations stack.


This means:
Roll the Trait Die twice for each applicable specialization.
Add all results together.


There is no cap on stacking unless the GM rules otherwise.

LIMITATIONS
Specializations:
Do not grant automatic success.
Do not bypass core action limits.
Do not override action economy unless another rule states so.
Must still fit within what can reasonably occur in one turn.


INTERACTION WITH REPEATED ATTEMPT PENALTY
The repeated attempt penalty still applies even if a specialization applies.
Specializations do not negate the −5 stacking penalty for retrying the same approach.

RESOLVE
Resolve represents a character’s ability to withstand harm, stress, and fatigue. It replaces traditional “hit points.”

Losing Resolve
Resolve is reduced when a character takes damage.
Damage may be:
Physical
Mental
Magical


When a character reaches 0 Resolve, the character falls Unconscious. While at 0 Resolve, the character may continue to accrue damage, as long as the damage type can reasonably be assumed to affect an unconscious creature.


If a character’s resolve reaches the negative of their maximum resolve, then the character dies.

A character at or below 0 Resolve may be stabilized by Magical or Mundane means.
Stabilization requires:
A successful roll
Difficulty set by the GM
Default difficulty: Average (16)


On success:
The character’s resolve equals 1, regardless of how far below zero it may have been previously.
The character remains unconscious.


REST & RECOVERY
One Rest Per Adventuring Day
During a Rest:
Recover half of missing Resolve
Recover all Resonance


Sleep (End of Adventuring Day)
During Sleep:
Recover all missing Resolve
Recover all Resonance
Remove one ongoing condition, unless the condition states otherwise


Ongoing Damage Restriction
If a character is suffering ongoing damage:
They cannot Rest
They cannot Sleep
They may, instead, spend the duration of the rest removing the ongoing damage effect, permitted that the GM allows the effect to be removed.

RESONANCE
Resonance is the magical resource used to enhance spells.

Casting foundational effects does not require resonance, but the player may choose to spend resonance to enhance the spell in various ways.

Spell resolution:
If the roll succeeds by less than 5, the player spends the appropriate Resonance
If the roll exceeds the difficulty by 5 or more, the player spends the appropriate Resonance and is then refunded 1 Resonance after the spell is resolved.
If the roll fails by 5 or less, the player spends the appropriate Resonance and the spell still occurs narratively but achieves an unintended result defined by the GM
If the roll fails by more than 5, the player spends the appropriate Resonance and the spell backfires in a way as determined by the GM


If Resonance Is Depleted
If a character has no Resonance remaining, They may spend Resolve instead, provided that doing so will not reduce the character to 0 resolve or below.


RESONANCE UPGRADES
Unless otherwise stated, multiple Resonance may be spent on a single casting.

Area
Increase diameter of affected area by 5’ per Resonance spent.
Example: 1×1 → 2×2 → 3×3 → etc.


Number of Targets
Each Resonance adds 1 additional target.


Damage
Each Resonance adds 1 additional damage die.


Duration
Duration follows a tiered progression. By default, foundational spells happen and resolve instantly, unless otherwise stated.

Each Resonance spent may upgrade the duration of any lasting effects by one step:
Until the end of target’s next turn
1 minute
10 minutes
1 hour
1 day
1 week
1 month
1 year
Permanent


Range
Range bands:
Melee
15 feet
30 feet
60 feet


Spending Resonance may upgrade the spell to the next range band.
Further increases beyond 60 feet are determined by GM discussion.

Accuracy
1 Resonance: +2 accuracy
2 Resonance: Advantage (roll d20 twice, take higher)


Speed (Casting Time Reduction)
Base rule: Casting a spell requires a Major Action
If a spell would normally take longer (minutes, hours, days, etc.):
Spending Resonance moves it one step down the reversed duration track until it reaches a Major Action. A Major Action spell may be cast as a Minor Adjustment by spending 1 further Resonance.


Force (Forced Movement)
If the spell already moves a creature, then the First upgrade doubles movement distance.
If the spell does not already move a creature, then the First upgrade adds 10 feet of movement.
Cost per application depends on the size of the creature being moved:
1 Resonance for 1×1 creature
2 Resonance for 2×2 creature
3 Resonance for 3×3 creature
etc.
Each additional application doubles movement distance:
10 → 20 → 40 → 80 → 160 feet, etc.


Permanence (Summoning)
3 Resonance: Summon lasts for battle
5 Resonance: Summon lasts for the day


Bypass Resistance
2 Resonance: Bypass resistance
4 Resonance: Bypass immunity


COMBAT STRUCTURE
Combat is structured as a series of rounds that are further broken down into turns.

TURN ORDER
The turn order of each round is decided collaboratively by the players.
Each player character and NPC takes exactly one turn per round, unless the GM specifies otherwise.
Certain creatures (e.g., bosses or multi-headed creatures) may take more than one turn per round at the GM’s discretion.
The character who acts last in a round automatically acts first in the next round.
If the character who acted last is an enemy, then they receive advantage on their first major action of the subsequent round.


STRUCTURE OF A TURN
Each turn consists of three parts:
Movement
Minor Adjustment
Major Action


These may occur in any order unless otherwise restricted.

MOVEMENT
A character may move up to their total movement speed during their turn.
Movement may be split up around other actions.
Movement cannot exceed total speed.


MELEE RANGE
Standard melee threat range is 5 feet (adjacent tiles). A character automatically threatens hostile creatures within melee range, unless the character is unaware of the hostile creature (i.e. the character is unconscious, or the hostile creature is hidden, etc.).

RANGED WEAPONS & RANGE BANDS
Ranged weapons may behave differently depending on the distance between the attacker and the target.  There are four main range bands to account for when using a ranged weapon:
Melee
Close (10 - 30 feet)
Far (30 - 60)
Maximum (60 feet or more depending on weapon)


Rules:
Ranged attacks in the Melee band are made with disadvantage.
Ranged attacks in close range behave as normal.
Ranged attacks at far range have disadvantage.
Ranged weapons cannot attack beyond their maximum effective range.


MINOR ADJUSTMENT
A player may convey interesting narrative flavor in exchange for receiving small mechanical advantages within their given situation. These mechanical advantages fall to the discretion of the GM to award, but the player should do their best to clarify the intent of their minor adjustment.


Examples:
Dipping a blade in poison so the player can further apply ongoing damage or status effects to a creature.
Swinging harder to increase potential damage at the cost of accuracy.
Preparing a stance that may trade off damage in exchange for greater defense.


MAJOR ACTION
The Major Action is the primary action taken on a turn. There are no predefined action lists. The player is limited only by the truth of their situation and the duration of the task. Major actions must reasonably fit within the time span of a single turn, representing a single beat in the midst of the scene.

Examples of major actions include:
A single attack
Cast a spell
Dash
Dodge
Manipulate an object


Major action rules do not apply if time is situationally irrelevant and the action can occur uninterrupted.

ATTACKS IN COMBAT
When attacking:
Roll: d20 + Trait Die + Trait Value − Weapon Accuracy Modifier + Other Modifiers
The attack hits If the result meets or exceeds the target’s Defense.


On a hit:
Damage = Weapon Die + Trait Die + Trait Value + Modifiers - Target’s Mitigation
Hit Damage can never be reduced below 1, unless otherwise noted by the GM.

AREA ATTACKS
Roll damage once.
Make separate attack rolls for each target in the area.


DAMAGE TYPES
Attacks target either:
Physical Defense
Mental Defense


SHIELDS IN COMBAT
Using a Shield improves the physical defense of the character, but restricts the types of weapons that the character can wield:
Small Shield: +1 Physical Defense
Medium Shield: +2 Physical Defense
Large Shield: +4 Physical Defense (requires two hands)


Characters must possess necessary and logical equipment in order to attempt a task unless they can imagine a reason that would bypass the need for such equipment as agreed to by the GM. Example:
A player cannot climb a rope if there is no rope.
The player may still attempt to climb — but must do so without a rope.


WEAPON ACCURACY
Weapons have an accuracy modifier ranging from +2 (most accurate) to −2 (least accurate). These categories apply to both melee and ranged weapons.


Weapon categories:
Simple: +2/+0 accuracy, d4 damage 
Light: +1/-3 accuracy, d6 damage
Standard: 0/-6 accuracy, d8 damage
Heavy: −1 accuracy, d10 damage, requires two hands unless trained
Brutal: −2 accuracy, d12 damage, requires two hands, applies minor adjustment to target


Unarmed attacks do not use a weapon die and have an accuracy of +4/+3.

ATTACK TYPE DETERMINATION
The type of attack (physical vs mental):
Is determined by intent.
Determines which Defense is targeted.
Determines which Mitigation applies.


WEAPONS
Weapons determine:
Damage die (volatility)
Accuracy modifier (reliability)
Category (used for training and shield restrictions)

WEAPON TRAINING (Class Modifier)
Weapon Training may be selected as a Class Mechanic Modifier.
Each instance:
Applies to one weapon category.
Grants +1 accuracy when wielding weapons within that category.
Grants +1 damage when wielding weapons within that category.
Must specify melee or ranged weapons. Just because you’re trained in using light melee weapons doesn’t mean you’re trained at using light ranged weapons…


Weapon training may apply multiple times, either to grant bonuses within more than one category or to grant larger bonuses within a single category.

SHIELD RESTRICTIONS
A player who chooses not to wield a shield may instead wield any two-handed weapon or up to two one-handed weapons.  In the event that the player chooses to dual wield, they may wield any one-handed weapon in either hand, even if those weapons are from different categories or have different ranges.  Dual wielding characters automatically 


Small Shield
+1 Physical Defense
May wield any 1-handed weapon with which the character is trained
May not wield brutal weapons, even if another ability allows the weapon to be wielded with a single hand

Medium Shield
+2 Physical Defense
May wield any 1-handed weapon with which the character is trained
May not wield brutal or heavy weapons, even if another ability allows the weapon to be wielded with a single hand

Large Shield
+4 Physical Defense
Requires two hands, or 1 hand if trained with large shields.
Cannot wield weapons unless trained with large shields, in which case the character may wield any simple or light weapon with which that character is trained (i.e. using a weapon with a large shield requires training in both the shield AND the weapon).


ARMOR
Armor adds a penalty to Physical Defense (the character is easier to hit due to the heavy, restrictive nature of the armor) and a bonus to Physical Mitigation (the armor protects the bearer from attacks that find purchase).
It does not affect Mental Defense or Mental Mitigation.
Only one armor may be worn at a time.
A player may wear narrative clothing and accessories, but these provide no mechanical benefit unless otherwise stated.


ARMOR TRAINING (Class Modifier)
Armor Training may be selected as a Class Mechanic Modifier.
Each instance:
Applies to one armor category.
Grants +1 to the armor’s defense modifier.

Training may result in armor increasing Physical Defense if the bonus becomes high enough to offset the baseline penalty.

Armor Categories
None: +0 Mitigation, +2 Defense Penalty
Light: +2 Mitigation, 0 Defense Penalty
Standard: +3 Mitigation, -1 Defense Penalty
Heavy: +4 Mitigation, -2 Defense Penalty
Bulwark: +6 Mitigation, -4 Defense Penalty

SHIELDS
Shields are a special defensive item separate from armor.
A character may wear:
One armor
One charm
One shield


Shields do not protect against mental attacks. There may be some reasonable exceptions to this rule, at the discretion of the GM. For example, if the application of the mental attack requires locking eyes with the target, and the target is hidden behind a shield, then it may be possible for the target to avoid any resulting mental damage from the attack.


CHARMS
Charms function similarly to armor, but for Mental Defense and Mental Mitigation.
Charms do not affect Physical Defense or Physical Mitigation.
Only one charm may be worn at a time.


CHARM TRAINING (Class Modifier)
Charm Training may be selected as a Class Mechanic Modifier.
Each instance:
Applies to one charm category.
Grants +1 to that charm’s defense modifier.

Training may result in a charm increasing Mental Defense if the bonus becomes high enough to offset the baseline penalty.

MAGIC SYSTEM
Magic is optional. A character may choose to use magic or not at character creation.

MAGICAL DOMAIN
A Magical Domain defines the thematic space of the character’s magic. The domain is a general description of the types of effects the character is capable of producing.


The player is not restricted to a “spell list”. Instead, it is up to the player, with the approval of the GM, to conduct their magic in any way that falls within their character’s chosen domain and is narratively justified.


FOUNDATIONAL EFFECTS
At Level 1
A magical character defines:
2 Foundational Effects


These effects:
Must fall within the chosen domain.
Must be relatively minor.
Must be approved by the GM.
Are typically (but not required to be):
One combat effect
One role-play effect


Foundational Effect Rules
Foundational effects do not require Resonance to use at baseline.
Foundational effects define the character’s core magical capabilities.
They must be:
Narrow enough to prevent universal power
Broad enough to allow multiple uses
Narrative use supersedes mechanical use.


Example:
 If a character can hurl a mote of flame, they can light a campfire without spending Resonance.

Additional Foundational Effects are gained at every level divisible by 3 (3, 6, 9, 12, etc.), as long as the character is magical.


SPELLCASTING
To cast a spell:
Declare intent.
The GM assigns a Difficulty.
Roll: d20 + Trait Die + Trait Value + Modifiers


No Resonance Remaining
If a character has no Resonance remaining, they may choose to spend Resolve instead. The decision to cast from Resolve must be made BEFORE the spell is cast. Unless otherwise approved by the GM, the player may not cast a spell that would reduce their resolve to 0 or below.

STACKING RESONANCE UPGRADES
Unless otherwise stated:
Upgrades may be stacked.
Multiple Resonance may be spent in a single casting.
All selected upgrades apply simultaneously.

NON-COMBAT RESOLUTION
Non-combat resolution governs:
Social interaction
Stealth
Investigation
Chases
Extended tasks
Environmental challenges
Narrative obstacles


OPPOSED ROLLS
Opposed rolls are used when another character is actively resisting the player’s action.
Examples may include:
Arm wrestling
Sneaking past an alert sentry
Persuading someone who is actively resisting
Competitive actions between characters
If an opposed roll is necessary, all involved parties make a roll using appropriate traits according to their determined method of “winning” the event.
The character with the highest roll determines the outcome of the event, and may even choose to lose if they deem it narratively appropriate or desirable.

STANDARD DIFFICULTY CHECKS
In some cases, another sentient target may be included in an event, but a standard difficulty check is still appropriate (rather than an opposed check). Examples of such an event include:
When the target is unaware (like sneaking past a creature who is not on active sentry duty)
The target is not actively resisting (like trying to move an unconscious creature).
The task is environmental or abstract (like picking a lock or pushing a boulder)


The GM assigns a Difficulty based on established benchmarks.


SPECIALIZED TOOLS
Basic equipment may be necessary to allows an attempt at a task. Such equipment provides no bonus to accomplishing the task. For example, if a door requires a key, then having the key does not make the door easier to unlock; having the key merely makes unlocking the door possible.


Other equipment may grant a bonus to the accomplishment of a task.  When such equipment is present and the character is trained to use it, applying the equipment grants the player advantage on their roll to accomplish the task. Unless otherwise noted, the circumstances in which the equipment provides such advantage must be defined when the equipment is acquired. Examples of specialized tools include:
Dedicated thieves’ tools for lockpicking.
A hooked climbing rope.
A compass used for navigation.

SOCIAL CONFLICT
Social conflict follows normal resolution rules,  The GM determines whether or not rolls in social conflicts require opposition or standard difficulties. Any trait may be used to determine the outcome of a social conflict, provided that the use of such trait is deemed appropriate by the GM.  Otherwise, the most common social traits include Charisma, Wisdom, and Knowledge.

EXTENDED TASKS
For complex or long-term objectives:
The GM determines:
Number of rolls required
Time required
Stakes for failure


Success may require:
Multiple cumulative successes
Sequential successes
Success within time constraints


Failure may:
Escalate consequences
Increase difficulty
Introduce new complications


DOWNTIME
Downtime is primarily narrative.  The events that occur during downtime are handled collaboratively by the players and the GM. During downtime, the GM may ask for rolls from the players if they wish to spend their downtime in the production or achievement of a task. In such an event, the GM determines the necessary number and difficulty of the players’ rolls.


GM AUTHORITY
The GM determines:
When a roll is required.
The relevant trait.
The Difficulty.
Whether the action is possible.
Whether a single roll or multiple rolls are required.


All non-combat resolution flows through GM adjudication guided by established difficulty benchmarks.
